# Screenplay: The Entire History of Tata (and the Diamond That Saved It)

*Times are estimates at ~150 wpm (≈16:30 of VO, plus diegetic pauses, sting and end screen ≈ 18 min); final times come
from the recorded VO. **Q** = the question the viewer is holding (PLAYBOOK §7.4). Labels per §9.4. Every fact →
`../claims.csv` (row id in brackets).*

**Picture modes.** **ARCHIVE**: real photos and documents, moving (parallax, push-in, highlighter).
**FOOTAGE**: real or licensed clips. **3D**: Three.js/Blender scenes (ILLUSTRATION). **INK**: drawn people, see below.
**MAP**: India (official boundary), routes and pins.

**INK figures (our take on Creator's stick-figure mode, 2026-10-24).** Hand-drawn bronze-ink line figures on charcoal
blueprint paper, drawn on with a 12 fps line boil, planted feet, IK arms, faces that change with every beat, a soft
ember glow where they are lit. Indian clothing and props per era: dhoti and turban, nine-yard saree, Parsi *pheta* cap,
mill cap, steelworker's cotton vest and tongs, 1990s office shirt, a family of four on a scooter. **Only anonymous,
everyday Indians** (investors in a queue, mill workers, steelworkers, a family, "you"): **never** Jamsetji, Dorabji,
Meherbai, J.R.D., Ratan or any real, named person (they appear only in real photographs), never Upcott (his line is
shown as a document). They act out the viewer's situation and the crowd's feeling. Ink figures can walk *into* real
archive photos (composited) so drawing and history meet.

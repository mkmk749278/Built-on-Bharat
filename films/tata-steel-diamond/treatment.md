# The Diamond That Saved Tata Steel: creative treatment

*Built on Bharat · Film 1 · Series: Empires of India · Creative director's pitch (PLAYBOOK §2.1)*
*Every fact below is a lead found in sources so far (`docs/research/topic-choice-2026-10-24/report.md`). Each is
verified in the full fact-check before a single line is recorded. Where sources disagree, the film says so.*

## Logline
A British railway chief reportedly offered to eat every pound of steel rail Indians could make. Eight thousand
ordinary Indians bet their savings that he was wrong. Seventeen years later, with the furnaces about to go cold and
wages unpaid, one couple walked into a bank with their most precious possession, a diamond, to keep India's first steel
plant alive.

## The feeling
- **Start:** dread and wonder. A fortune on a bank counter, a plant in a jungle, thousands of families waiting for wages.
- **Low point:** the man who dreamed it is dead, the money is gone, the doubters look right.
- **End:** a quiet, proud lump in the throat. The steel in the bridge you crossed today started with people who
  refused to let a dream die.

## The angle (three considered, one chosen)
1. *"How Tata Steel was built"* (a company history in date order). **Rejected:** that's what the case studies do.
2. *"The British said Indians couldn't make steel"* (a revenge story). **Rejected:** easy anger, and it makes the
   British the main character.
3. **Chosen: "the diamond"**, a story of what Indians were willing to risk. The diamond is the hook, the 1907
   investors are the heart, and the furnace is the mechanism. It is a bet whose ending the viewer wants to see.

## Structure (about 12–14 minutes; the topic decides)
| | Beat | The viewer's question |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold open** 0:00 | Bombay, 1924. A bank. A velvet case opens; furnace-orange light on the table. Thousands of workers in a jungle town are owed wages. *Reconstruction.* | What is in the case, and why give it away? |
| **The promise** 0:15 | It is a diamond, and the only thing between India's first steel plant and its death. | Did it work? |
| **Open loop** 0:40 | "And what happened to that diamond afterwards is the best part of this story. I'll show you at the end." | (held to the end) |
| **1. The dream** | A Parsi trader in Manchester watches iron become power. J.N. Tata decides India must make its own steel, and spends decades searching, then dies in 1904, never having seen the plant. | Who carries the dream now? |
| **2. The letter from the jungle** | Geologist P.N. Bose's 1904 letter about iron ore at Gorumahisani. Surveyors in sal forest, two rivers, a village called Sakchi. | Why build a steel plant in a jungle? |
| **3. Eight thousand Indians** | London money won't come. In 1907 the company goes to the Indian public, and about 8,000 Indians put up about Rs 2.32 crore. The swadeshi mood: share forms, queues, newspapers. *Reaction beat.* | Will their bet pay? |
| **4. The furnace** *(mechanism chapter)* | A jungle becomes a works. **3D cutaway of a blast furnace:** ore, coke and limestone in, molten iron out. First ingot 1912. Upcott's reported boast; the war; rails shipped abroad. | Did they prove him wrong? |
| **5. The crash** | After the war: prices fall, debts come due, wages can't be paid. The darkest moment. | Is this the end? |
| **6. The diamond** | 1924: Sir Dorabji and Lady Meherbai pledge the Jubilee Diamond and their jewellery to the Imperial Bank. Wages are paid. The furnaces stay lit. *1.5 s of silence, then the thud.* | And then? |
| **7. The payoff** | The plant survives and grows; Jamshedpur becomes a planned steel city (and, if verified, a pioneer of workers' welfare). | What happened to the diamond? |
| **Ending** | The loop closes: what the diamond became after it left the bank (verify before writing). Then today: the steel in Indian bridges, trains and buildings. One question for the comments. Outro sting. | — |

## Signature moments
1. **The match cut.** The diamond's facets catch furnace light on the bank table → hard cut on the same orange to
   molten steel pouring in 1912. Two kinds of fire, one bet.
2. **The furnace cutaway.** A hero 3D section of a blast furnace, built from real drawings of the period, ore falling
   through layers of heat (labelled ILLUSTRATION). The mechanism finally clicks.
3. **The jungle becomes a city.** One continuous push from a 1900s survey map (two rivers, forest, a village) into
   today's satellite view of Jamshedpur, the rivers held in place as the city grows around them.
4. **Eight thousand names.** A share register (real if we can find it, a labelled reconstruction if not) scrolls
   past, faster and faster, as the counter climbs to Rs 2.32 crore.

## The visual world
Inside the brand's charcoal and bronze: **furnace orange and soot black**, sepia archive photographs lit as if by
firelight, blueprint drawings of the plant, paper share certificates and ledgers on an isometric desk. The camera
moves like a steelworker: slow and heavy, then sudden. Sparks only where metal is really hot.

## Sound palette
Low furnace roar, the clang of metal, steam whistles from period railway recordings, a ledger page turning.
Score: a slow cello and low strings for the dream, tabla and a pulse for the build, everything stopping for the bank
scene, a warm lift with a solo bansuri at the end.

## The package (written first, PLAYBOOK §13.2)
- **Viewer:** a 20–35-year-old Indian anywhere in the country who watches business and history explainers, has heard
  of Tata, and has never heard this story. Also, everyone in Jamshedpur.
- **Search phrases:** main "tata steel history"; secondary "jamshedpur history", "jamsetji tata story", "how tata
  steel started", "jubilee diamond".
- **Title (main):** `The Diamond That Saved Tata Steel` (34 characters; the subject and the curiosity in one line)
- **Alternatives:** `Tata Steel History: The Diamond That Saved It` ·
  `How 8,000 Indians Built Tata Steel (and a Diamond Saved It)`
- **Thumbnail concepts (3 to build and test):**
  1. A huge, furnace-lit diamond held in a worker's sooty hand; words: **HER DIAMOND**.
  2. A steel rail on a dinner plate with a fork; words: **"I'LL EAT IT"** (only if Upcott's line is verified and
     attributed in the film).
  3. Split: a 1900s jungle on the left, a glowing steel plant on the right; words: **1907: JUNGLE**.

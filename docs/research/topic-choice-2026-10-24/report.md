# First film: which topic? (research, 2026-10-24)

**Answer.** Film 1 is **Tata Steel and Jamshedpur**, told through one object: the diamond a couple handed to a bank in
1924 to keep India's first steel plant alive. It has proven demand, no cinematic English film yet, the strongest fit
with our brand world (furnaces, sparks, metal, blueprints), and the most Indian humanity of all 26 candidates: eight
thousand ordinary Indians who bet their savings on it, the founder who never lived to see it, the workers who built a
city in a jungle. It opens a series, *Empires of India*, with Amul and the Taj to follow.

## How this was measured
`scripts/yt_demand.py` on 26 candidate topics (`topics.tsv`): YouTube autocomplete for India (`gl=IN`, `hl=en`, 4 seed
phrases a topic, about 900 phrases in all) for **demand**, and the top 20 YouTube search results for one main query a
topic (views, age, length, channel) for **supply**. Raw files: `raw/<slug>.json`, `summary.tsv`. Read 2026-10-24.
**Limits:** results show total views, not views a month; autocomplete shows *that* people type a phrase, not how
many; the Hindi/English split was judged by channel (many Hindi channels use English titles). Study data only.

## Why Tata Steel wins
1. **Proven demand, open English space.** People search the history in many languages, including "in english". Hindi
   channels hold the big numbers; the one English film is a 4-year-old case study, not a story.
2. **The best curiosity hook of all 26:** a diamond, a steel plant and a deadline. Nobody leads with it.
3. **Indian humanity on every page** (leads checked so far, see below): ordinary Indians funding it in weeks when
   London wouldn't; a scientist's letter from the jungle; workers who built a city; a couple who pledged their jewels
   to pay wages.
4. **Respect and pride with honesty:** the British doubted it, Indians built it, it nearly failed, it survived.
5. **Brand fit:** our sting is a medallion forged in sparks. The film's world is the furnace.
6. **Series start:** *Empires of India*. Amul (film 2) and the Taj (film 3) follow.

## Leads checked so far (to be fully verified in the fact-check, PLAYBOOK §9)
- P.N. Bose found iron ore at Gorumahisani (Mayurbhanj) and wrote to J.N. Tata on 24 February 1904; Tata Steel's own
  materials call the letter the trigger for the Sakchi plant. (Tata Steel press releases; Wikipedia, P.N. Bose)
- Tata Iron and Steel Company registered 26 August 1907; about 8,000 Indian investors raised about Rs 2.32 crore
  (three weeks per Tata Steel's material; eight weeks per one encyclopedia: **conflict to resolve**).
  (tatasteel.com annual report 2006-07 heritage page; encyclopedia.com)
- First pig iron December 1911; first steel ingot 1912. (Tata Steel heritage timeline; GKToday)
- Sir Frederick Upcott reportedly offered to "eat every pound" of steel rail Indians made (Time; Tata Investment).
  **Treat as reported, attribute, find the earliest source.**
- 1924 cash crisis: Sir Dorabji Tata pledged his wife Lady Meherbai's Jubilee Diamond and other jewellery to the
  Imperial Bank of India for a loan to keep the company running (R.M. Lala and Gita Piramal as cited; Live History
  India; Wikipedia, Jubilee Diamond). **Amount differs by source (Rs 1 crore vs about Rs 2 crore): resolve from Lala.**
- The diamond was later sold and the money went to the Sir Dorabji Tata Trust (several sources). What the trust then
  built is a possible ending: **verify before use.**

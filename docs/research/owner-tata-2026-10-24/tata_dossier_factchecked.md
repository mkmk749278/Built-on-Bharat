# The ENTIRE History of Tata in 20 Minutes: Research Dossier for *Built on Bharat* (Fact-Checked to 24 October 2026)

The Tata story holds up as a documentary spine, but several of its best-known "cinematic" beats are legends or company-sourced numbers. The Watson's Hotel snub has no contemporary evidence. [tripprof](https://tripprof.com/en/blog/hotels-with-incredible-backstories/) The Upcott "eat every pound" line comes only from Tata-authored histories. The ₹2.3 crore steel float is real, but "three weeks" is one witness's account. Tata's "over 70%" EV share is three years out of date: it was 39.2% in FY26 and about 42% in September 2026. [vahanbazaar](https://vahanbazaar.in/news/india-ev-sales-fy2026-84-percent-surge-tata-mahindra-mg) [autofystore](https://autofystore.com/blogs/featured-blogs/electric-car-sales-september-2026-brand-wise-india) The modern story is also darker than the PR version. Air India lost ₹22,238 crore in FY26 after the AI171 crash. [whalesbook](https://www.whalesbook.com/news/English/economy/Air-India-FY26-Loss-Doubles-to-Rs-22238-Crore/6aaad0ceed7f8e81734dfeca) The Dholera fab's opening node and timeline are contested. [techtimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320824/20260717/indias-first-chip-fab-opens-90nm-two-process-nodes-below-what-tata-promised.htm) And in September 2026 Tata Trusts and Tata Sons went into open conflict over the chairman's reappointment and an RBI-forced listing. [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-20/tata-trust-restates-chandra-s-reappointment-to-tata-sons-is-void) [businessmodelanalyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/tata-sons-listing-trusts-veto-special-resolution/)

## TL;DR
- **Hard core, soft edges:** The ₹21,000 start (1868), Empress Mills (1877), TISCO's ₹2.32 crore Indian-subscribed float (1907), the ₹30 lakh IISc endowment, JRD's 1932 flight, Air India's 1953 nationalisation and 2022 return, and the JLR, Corus and Tetley deals are all verifiable. The Watson's Hotel origin story is best treated as a myth. The Upcott quote, "three weeks", "1,500 miles of rail", "half his wealth" and "India's first electrified building" are company-sourced or overstated, so label them on screen.
- **The modern numbers cut against hero worship:**
  - Tata EV share fell from 84% (FY23) to 53.4% (FY25) to 39.2% (FY26), recovering to about 42% by mid-2026.
  - Air India's consolidated loss doubled to ₹22,238 crore in FY26.
  - Cyrus Mistry's October 2016 letter to the Tata Sons board said the Nano "has consistently lost money, peaking at Rs.1,000 crores," and Port Talbot's blast furnaces were shut in 2024.
  - TCS supplied about 87% of Tata Sons' ₹32,528 crore FY26 dividend income.
- **The ending writes itself, but it is unresolved:**
  - Tata Trusts own about 66% of Tata Sons, and Shapoorji Pallonji owns 18.37%.
  - The RBI refused Tata Sons' exit from listing rules on 11 September 2026.
  - On 17 September 2026 the Tata Sons board voted 4–1 to reappoint N. Chandrasekaran over Noel Tata's objection, and the Trusts call that vote "void ab initio."
  - Frame the film's finale as an open question, not a triumph.

## Key Findings

- **Strongest verified arc for the script:** Indian capital built steel when London would not finance it, then supplied the British war effort, and a century later bought Corus. [aurosociety +2](https://fdi.aurosociety.org/sustainability/corporate-responsibility/building-of-the-steel-city/) The money and the war supply are well documented. Only the Upcott taunt is shaky.
- **Most dangerous retellings to repeat uncritically:**
  - Watson's Hotel: historians Sharada Dwivedi and Charles Allen call it too petty a motive, and no contemporary record exists. [scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1044517/why-did-jamsetji-nusserwanji-tata-build-the-taj-mahal-hotel-in-bombay) [tripprof](https://tripprof.com/en/blog/hotels-with-incredible-backstories/)
  - The "IISc was Vivekananda's idea" framing. Tata's letter to Vivekananda is real (23 November 1898), but Tata's plan predates it.
  - "Over 70% EV share." This was true around FY22–FY23 and is false today. [vahanbazaar](https://vahanbazaar.in/news/india-ev-sales-fy2026-84-percent-surge-tata-mahindra-mg) [business-standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/tata-motors-eyes-market-share-gains-cv-pv-businesses-125060901235_1.html)
- **Best-documented "redemption" beat:** the 1999 Ford humiliation and the 2008 "favour." Both come from one named Tata insider, Pravin Kadle, speaking in 2015. It is a credible single-source anecdote. Present it as "according to Tata veteran Pravin Kadle."
- **Biggest live story (October 2026):** Tata's ownership model, built so that charity owns the empire, is under the most strain in its history. Ratan Tata's death (9 October 2024) removed the referee. [businessmodelanalyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/tata-sons-listing-trusts-veto-special-resolution/) Since then:
  - A trustee was voted out (Mehli Mistry, October 2025). [finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/noel-tata-tata-group-leadership-authority-2026)
  - The Tata Sons AGM failed for lack of quorum (18 August 2026). [businessmodelanalyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/tata-sons-listing-trusts-veto-special-resolution/)
  - The RBI denied Tata Sons' bid to exit listing rules. [businessmodelanalyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/tata-sons-listing-trusts-veto-special-resolution/)
  - The board and the Trusts publicly dispute whether the chairman was validly reappointed. [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-20/tata-trust-restates-chandra-s-reappointment-to-tata-sons-is-void) [thestatesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/business/tata-sons-rejects-noel-tatas-objections-against-chandrasekarans-reappointment-1503642696.html/amp)

## MODULE 1: Colonial Genesis and the Labour Innovation (1868–1904)

**1. Capital and context.**
- Jamsetji Nusserwanji Tata (born Navsari, 3 March 1839) joined his father's trading and banking business in 1859. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamsetji_Tata)
- In 1868 he founded his own trading firm with ₹21,000 (company-sourced).
- His early fortune came from trade, including the China trade, where opium and cotton were the standard Parsi export goods. Handle this delicately: the opium link is part of the period's commerce, not a Tata-specific scandal, but an honest film should name it.
- The economic backdrop was the colonial loop. India exported raw cotton to Lancashire and imported finished cloth.
- **Script line:** Jamsetji's move was to capture the value-addition step at home.

**2. Empress Mills (company floated 1874; opened 1 January 1877).**
- He floated the Central India Spinning, Weaving and Manufacturing Company in 1874.
- The mill opened on the day Queen Victoria was proclaimed Empress of India, hence "Empress Mills." [dwarkaparichay](https://dwarkaparichay.com/blog/jamsetji-tata-father-of-indian-industry/)
- **Site logic (Tata heritage accounts and the Catalign history):**
  - He deliberately built "where the cotton came from" rather than in Bombay or Ahmedabad, which were then "the Lancashires of India."
  - A first proposal in Jabalpur was turned down; Nagpur offered suitable land. [catalign](https://www.catalign.in/2010/06/innovations-in-jamsetji-tatas-empress.html)
  - Nagpur was a cotton belt with a rail link to Bombay and, compared with Bombay, cheaper land.
- **Caveat:** these "rail junction and cheap land" rationales are standard retellings rather than quotes from Jamsetji.

**3. Labour reforms: what happened under Jamsetji vs. later.**

| Measure | Date | Under Jamsetji? | Source quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pension fund | 1886 (some sources say 1887; one says provident fund 1886, pension 1887) | Yes | Tata.com and multiple secondary sources. Flag the one-year discrepancy. [tata](https://www.tata.com/newsroom/people-first-labour-welfare) [catalign](https://www.catalign.in/2010/06/innovations-in-jamsetji-tatas-empress.html) |
| Accident compensation | 1895 | Yes | Tata.com |
| Humidifiers, sprinklers, water filtration, ventilated sheds, grain depot, dispensary | 1880s–1890s | Yes (mill technology plus welfare) | Tata heritage |
| Crèches and a women's dispensary with a lady doctor | Unclear; likely later | Uncertain | Tata.com describes these without firm dates [tata](https://www.tata.com/newsroom/heritage/jamsetji-women-empowerment-tata-group) |
| Maternity benefit (Empress Mills, "first in Central Provinces") | Undated in Tata sources | Uncertain | Tata.com |
| 8-hour day, leave with pay, maternity benefit (Tata Steel) | 1912; 1920; **1928** | **No.** These came under Dorabji and his successors at TISCO | JETIR paper: Tata Steel maternity scheme 1928; India's Maternity Benefit Act 1946 [jetir](https://www.jetir.org/papers/JETIR1905X85.pdf) |

- **Jamsetji's own rationale (1895 speech at the Empress Mills extension):** "We do not claim to be more unselfish, more generous, or more philanthropic than other people. But, we think, we started on sound and straightforward business principles, considering the interests of our shareholders our own, and the health and welfare of our employees the sure foundation of our prosperity." [catalign](https://www.catalign.in/2010/06/innovations-in-jamsetji-tatas-empress.html)
- That is the verified version of "healthy workers make better cloth." **Do not use the paraphrase as a quote.**
- **"Decades ahead of European labour movements" is overstated.** Bismarck's Germany legislated accident insurance in 1884 and old-age pensions in 1889. "Ahead of Indian law and most British-Indian mills" is defensible.

**4. Watson's Hotel and the Taj.**
- Watson's Hotel (Esplanade Mansion) was prefabricated in England and built in 1867–69. It was a Europeans-only hotel and India's oldest surviving cast-iron building. [eduindex](https://eduindex.org/2020/09/26/watson-hotel/) [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson's_Hotel)
- The Tata snub is undocumented (see Myth table, row 2).
- **Taj facts:**
  - Construction began in 1898; the hotel opened 16 December 1903, months before Jamsetji died at Bad Nauheim on 19 May 1904. [tata +3](https://www.tata.com/newsroom/heritage/jamsetji-tata-perseverance-iisc)
  - Original architects were Sitaram Khanderao Vaidya and D. N. Mirza; builder Khansaheb Sorabji Ruttonji Contractor. [theculturetrip](https://theculturetrip.com/asia/india/articles/the-history-of-mumbais-taj-mahal-palace-hotel-in-1-minute)
  - Indo-Saracenic style with a central dome. [theculturetrip](https://theculturetrip.com/asia/india/articles/the-history-of-mumbais-taj-mahal-palace-hotel-in-1-minute)
  - Imported fittings: American fans, German elevators, Turkish baths, and spun-iron pillars from Paris that still hold up the ballroom. [theculturetrip](https://theculturetrip.com/asia/india/articles/the-history-of-mumbais-taj-mahal-palace-hotel-in-1-minute)
- **Recommended narration:** "The legend says he was turned away from a whites-only hotel. Historians say there's no record of that. What is recorded is the hotel he built, and who it let in."

## MODULE 2: The Three Pillars — Steel, Power, Science

**1. Finding the ore.**
- **Chanda dead end:** Jamsetji had read German geologist Ritter von Schwartz's report pointing to Chanda district (Central Provinces). Lohara ore and Warora coal proved unsuitable. [tatasteel100](http://www.tatasteel100.com/people/feel-for-steel.asp) [india](https://www.india.com/business/tata-group-iron-business-one-letter-by-pn-bose-to-jamsetji-tata-created-history-know-the-untold-story-of-tata-steel-7652326/)
- **US expertise:** in 1902 Jamsetji met Julian Kennedy in Pittsburgh, who recommended consulting engineer Charles Page Perin. [avenuemail](https://avenuemail.in/the-founding-story-of-tata-steel-jamshedpur/)
- **The find:** in 1904, geologist P. N. Bose, then with the Mayurbhanj state, identified rich ore in the Gorumahisani hills and wrote to Tata. That letter is the pivotal document. [india](https://www.india.com/business/tata-group-iron-business-one-letter-by-pn-bose-to-jamsetji-tata-created-history-know-the-untold-story-of-tata-steel-7652326/)
- **The site:** the Perin–Weld survey, with Dorabji Tata and Srinivas Rao, chose Sakchi, near the Subarnarekha–Kharkai confluence and close to ore, coal and water.
- **Ore grade:** the "60%+ hematite" figure is widely repeated but was not verified in this research. Use "high-grade hematite."
- **C. M. Weld on Jamsetji's motive (quoted by Tata):** his plan "was inspired by something far broader and deeper than the mere hope of adding to his fortune." [tata](https://www.tata.com/newsroom/business/tata-steel-india-75-years-independence)

**2. London fails, India subscribes.**
- The company was initially registered in England in 1906 but met a lukewarm response there. [avenuemail](https://avenuemail.in/the-founding-story-of-tata-steel-jamshedpur/)
- It was re-registered in India on 26 August 1907 with capital of ₹2,31,75,000 (about ₹2.32 crore). [avenuemail](https://avenuemail.in/the-founding-story-of-tata-steel-jamshedpur/)
- A Swadeshi-era public appeal followed. Axel Sahlin (quoted by Tata): "Old and young, rich and poor, men and women, they came, offering their mites … at the end of three weeks, the entire capital required … £1,630,000 — was secured, every penny contributed by some 8,000 native Indians." [tata](https://www.tata.com/newsroom/business/tata-steel-india-75-years-independence)
- The Tatas reportedly retained about 11%. [company-histories](https://www.company-histories.com/Tata-Iron-Steel-Co-Ltd-Company-History.html)
- Construction began 27 February 1908. Pig iron came in 1911 [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jamshedpur) and steel in 1912.

**3. War and the city.**
- In WWI, TISCO reportedly supplied about 1,500 miles of rail and 300,000 tonnes of steel at concessional rates (company-sourced). [tatasteel100](http://www.tatasteel100.com/people/feel-for-steel.asp)
- The 1917 Howrah Bridge claim of "80,000 tonnes" also comes from Tata's site. [tatasteel100](http://www.tatasteel100.com/people/feel-for-steel.asp) The current Howrah Bridge was actually built in 1936–42, so treat that line cautiously.
- A viceregal statement quoted in Tata histories: "I can hardly imagine what we should have done during these four years if the Tata company had not been able to give us steel rails … not only for Mesopotamia but for Egypt, Palestine and East Africa." [aurosociety](https://fdi.aurosociety.org/sustainability/corporate-responsibility/building-of-the-steel-city/)
- Jamshedpur was a planned company town: 6,000 people by 1910, free medical services for employees from 1915, and the Jamshedpur Technical Institute in 1921. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jamshedpur)
- **Labour unrest was real.** There was a 1922 strike; film it as well as the welfare story. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jamshedpur)
- "Asia's first planned industrial city" is a common but unverified superlative. Prefer "one of India's first planned industrial cities."

**4. Hydro and science.**
- **Hydro:** Tata Hydroelectric Power Supply Company was formed in 1910. Monsoon-fed Western Ghats lakes (Lonavla, Walwhan, Shirawta) drove the Khopoli station, supplying Bombay's mills.
  - Tata Power's own site confirms only the year: "Commissioned in 1915, the 72 MW Khopoli station marked the beginning of Tata Power's hydro journey." The commonly cited exact date (8 February 1915) remains unconfirmed, so verify it with Tata Central Archives.
- **Science:**
  - **Endowment:** ₹30 lakh (14 buildings plus 4 landed properties, announced September 1898). The plan went to Lord Curzon on 31 December 1898. [theprint](https://theprint.in/feature/around-town/iisc-gave-bengaluru-science-hub-tag-story-of-birth-yet-to-be-written/2355214/) [catalign](https://www.catalign.in/2010/02/jamsetji-tatas-vision-of-university-of.html)
  - **Mysore's contribution:** 371 acres in Bangalore, ₹5 lakh, and an annual grant (later reduced). [awazthevoice](https://www.awazthevoice.in/heritage-news/j-n-tata-established-iisc-on-the-advice-of-vivekananda-35060.html)
  - **Founding:** IISc was established by vesting order on 27 May 1909 and opened to 24 students in 1911. Jamsetji never saw it. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Trusts)
  - **Jamsetji's endowment yield:** IISc's origin document records ₹1.25 lakh a year from the endowment against ₹87,500 a year from the government. [iisc](https://www.iisc.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/ORIGIN.pdf)

## MODULE 3: JRD, Air India and the Licence Raj (1932–1991)

**1. Aviation pioneer.**
- JRD received India's first pilot licence on 10 February 1929. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JRD_Tata)
- On 15 October 1932 he flew a de Havilland Puss Moth carrying mail from Karachi (Drigh Road) via Ahmedabad to Bombay (Juhu). [khaleejtimes](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/long-reads/an-aviator-and-a-perfectionist) [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehangir_Ratanji_Dadabhoy_Tata)
- Tata Airlines became Air-India in 1946.
- Air-India International was launched in 1948 in partnership with the government. [business-standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/companies/the-tatas-flight-story-113022000585_1.html)

**2. The luxury standard.** These are standard aviation-history facts, but they were not re-verified in this research; check them against Air India and Tata archives before narrating.
- The Maharajah mascot (1946, Bobby Kooka and Umesh Rao).
- Boeing 707 induction in 1960.
- JRD's obsessive service standards. His line: "If you want excellence, you must aim at perfection." [khaleejtimes](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/long-reads/an-aviator-and-a-perfectionist)

**3. 1953 nationalisation.**
- The Air Corporations Act 1953 split aviation into Air India (international) and Indian Airlines (domestic).
- JRD had himself suggested a two-carrier consolidation but opposed the takeover. [business-standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/companies/the-tatas-flight-story-113022000585_1.html)
- He accepted the chairmanship of Air India and remained unpaid for 25 years. [markhub24](https://www.markhub24.com/post/he-flew-the-first-plane-his-family-bought-it-back-68-years-later-the-extraordinary-story-of-air-in)
- **Removal:** a month after the 1 January 1978 Air India 747 crash off Bombay (213 killed), the Desai government dropped him. JRD learned of it from his successor, Air Chief Marshal P. C. Lal, on 3 February 1978. [scroll](https://scroll.in/article/904431/how-the-government-took-air-india-from-jrd-tata-asked-him-to-run-it-and-then-took-it-away-again)

**4. Licence Raj ethics.**
- TELCO's 1954 Daimler-Benz truck collaboration and Tata Chemicals (Mithapur, 1939) grew under capacity licensing and price controls. The MRTP Act of 1969 capped large houses' expansion.
- The best-documented "no bribes" episode is later, in the 1990s. The Tata–Singapore Airlines domestic airline proposal stalled for years.
  - Speaking in Dehradun on 15 November 2010 (PTI; Business Standard, 16 November 2010), Ratan Tata said a fellow industrialist had told him "the minister wanted Rs 15 crore."
  - He did not name the minister, and said: "I didn't want to go to bed at night thinking I got an airline by paying for it."
  - Former aviation minister C. M. Ibrahim (1996–97) denied it and demanded that Tata name the minister.
- This is Ratan Tata's own claim, not adjudicated. Present it as attributed.
- **Avoid blanket "Tata never paid a bribe" claims.** They are unprovable.

**5. TCS (1968).**
- Founded as Tata Computer Systems, a division of Tata Sons, in 1968. F. C. Kohli took charge in 1969.
- Early work was punched-card data processing for group companies.
- It was listed in 2004.
- **Today it is the group's cash engine:** in FY26 Tata Sons collected ₹32,528 crore in dividends, about 87% of it from TCS (Business Model Analyst, citing the Tata Sons annual report). [businessmodelanalyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/tata-sons-listing-trusts-veto-special-resolution/)

## MODULE 4: Ratan Tata — Satraps, Globalisation, Missteps (1991–2012)

**1. The satrap purge.**
- Ratan Tata succeeded JRD in 1991, the year of India's balance-of-payments crisis.
- He moved against entrenched company chiefs: Russi Mody (Tata Steel), Darbari Seth (Tata Chemicals) and Ajit Kerkar (Indian Hotels).
- The main tools were a retirement-age rule and raising Tata Sons' stakes in group companies.
- Ratan Tata himself stepped down at 75 (28 December 2012), consistent with that rule. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratan_Tata)
- Exact ouster dates and the retirement-age details were not re-fetched here. Verify with Tata Central Archives or Morarji/Lala biographies.

**2. Indica (1998) and the Ford meeting.**
- The Indica was India's first fully indigenous passenger car. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Motors_Passenger_Vehicles)
- It drew about 115,000 initial bookings. Sources disagree on the window ("within a week," "within two weeks," or a 17–23 January 1999 booking round), so use "about 115,000 bookings."
- Early complaints about power and quality followed and led to the "Refreshingly New Indica V2."
- **The Detroit meeting (1999), per Pravin Kadle (PTI/Business Standard, March 2015):** Ford executives said, "you do not know anything, why did you start the passenger car division at all … they will do us a favour by buying our car division." Ratan Tata was "sombre through the 90-minute flight" back to New York. [business-standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/when-humiliated-tata-did-favour-to-ford-with-jlr-buyout-115031500207_1.html)
- Single-source; label it as such.

**3. JLR (2008).**
- Announced 26 March 2008 at $2.3 billion (some sources say $2.23 billion); closed 2 June 2008, just before the global crisis. [slideshare +2](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/presentation1-5623640/5623640)
- Kadle says Bill Ford told Tata, "You are doing us a big favour by buying JLR." Ford has not confirmed this. [easterneye](https://www.easterneye.biz/ratan-tata-jlr-jaguar-land-rover-ford/)
- The Evoque and the new Defender drove the revival.
- **Recent shock:** the September 2025 cyberattack halted JLR production for weeks. Wikipedia's account notes TCS managed JLR's cybersecurity, which makes it a reputational knot for the group. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_Land_Rover)
- JLR financials were not re-verified in this research.

**4. Tetley and Corus.**
- **Tetley (2000):** a £271 million ($432 million) leveraged buyout from Schroders Private Equity Fund. Gale's *International Directory of Company Histories*, citing the *Economic Times* (28 February 2000), calls it "the largest takeover of a foreign company by an Indian one to date."
- **Corus:** won on 31 January 2007 at 608p per share after an auction against CSN, valuing Corus at £6.7 billion (about $12 billion). - **The cost:**

  - Tata Steel UK's FY24 pre-tax loss was £1.12 billion, including £625 million of restructuring and impairment charges. [gmk](https://gmk.center/en/news/tata-steels-losses-in-the-uk-reached-1-1-billion-due-to-the-closure-of-the-port-talbot-blast-furnace/)

[gmk](https://gmk.center/en/news/tata-steel-has-shut-down-the-last-blast-furnace-at-port-talbot-plant/) [senedd](https://research.senedd.wales/research-articles/one-year-on-how-has-the-blast-furnaces-closing-affected-port-talbot/)

[gmk](https://gmk.center/en/news/tata-steel-has-shut-down-the-last-blast-furnace-at-port-talbot-plant/) [business-standard](https://www.business-standard.com/specials/news/port-talbot-s-green-furnace-marks-end-of-costly-firefighting-for-tata-steel-125072000782_1.html)

**5. The Nano.**
- Ratan Tata's ₹1 lakh "people's car" was unveiled January 2008 and launched in 2009. [drivespark](https://www.drivespark.com/four-wheelers/2018/tata-nano-production-stop-soon-one-unit-produced-in-june-three-sold-025968.html) [tribuneindia](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/tata-nano-ends-2019-with-zero-output-sale-of-1-unit-22766)
- **Singur:** a 997-acre acquisition in May 2006 sparked protests led by Mamata Banerjee. Tata announced its exit in October 2008 and moved to Sanand, Gujarat, after Narendra Modi's one-word SMS: "Welcome." The Sanand plant cost about ₹2,000 crore and its first car rolled out in June 2010. [tribuneindia](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/welcome-sms-that-drove-nano-from-bengal-to-gujarat/amp) [deccanherald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fwest-bengal%2Fbengals-singur-saga-a-timeline-of-tata-motors-nano-project-2750018)
- **Sales collapse:**
  - About 203,000 initial bookings, of which 100,000 were allotted by lottery.
  - Monthly sales peaked at about 9,000 (July 2010), then fell to 509 in November 2010 amid fire incidents and safety concerns. [slideshare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/positioning-iof-tata-nano/27674490)
  - Sales were 7,591 in FY17; one car was produced in June 2018; production halted in 2018. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Nano) [rushlane](https://www.rushlane.com/tata-nano-journey-comes-to-an-end-12257501.html)
- **Admitted mistakes:** Tata itself admitted the error of marketing it as "the cheapest car." [theweek](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2019/10/08/no-tata-nano-production-first-9-months-2019-1-unit-sold.html)
- **Losses and compensation:**
  - Cyrus Mistry's leaked October 2016 letter to the Tata Sons board (reported by Just Auto and Business Standard, 27 October 2016) said the Nano "has consistently lost money, peaking at Rs.1,000 crores. As there is no line of sight to profitability for the Nano, any turnaround strategy for the company requires to shut it down."
  - In a coda, an arbitral tribunal on 30 October 2023 awarded Tata Motors ₹765.78 crore plus 11% annual interest from 1 September 2016 against West Bengal's WBIDC.

## MODULE 6: Ownership — The Forbes Paradox

- **Why no Tata tops a rich list:** the Trusts, not family members, own the empire. Seven Tata Trusts hold about 66% of Tata Sons: [businessmodelanalyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/tata-sons-listing-trusts-veto-special-resolution/)
  - Sir Dorabji Tata Trust: 27.98%. [barandbench](https://barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/public-trustee-regime-and-tata-trusts-an-old-law-a-contemporary-question)
  - Sir Ratan Tata Trust: 23.56%. [barandbench](https://barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/public-trustee-regime-and-tata-trusts-an-old-law-a-contemporary-question)
  - Those two together: 51.54%. [businessmodelanalyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/tata-sons-listing-trusts-veto-special-resolution/)
  - Shapoorji Pallonji (Mistry family): 18.37%. [businessmodelanalyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/tata-sons-listing-trusts-veto-special-resolution/)
  - Group companies: about 13%. [businessmodelanalyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/tata-sons-listing-trusts-veto-special-resolution/)
  - Noel Tata personally: about 1%. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Sons)
- **Control is contractual, not just equity.** Tata Sons' Articles give the two main Trusts, while they hold at least 40%: [businessmodelanalyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/tata-sons-listing-trusts-veto-special-resolution/)
  - a mandatory joint nominee for any shareholder meeting to be constituted (Article 86); [businessmodelanalyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/tata-sons-listing-trusts-veto-special-resolution/)
  - nomination of one-third of the board (Article 104B); [businessmodelanalyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/tata-sons-listing-trusts-veto-special-resolution/)

  - Ratan Tata died 9 October 2024; Noel Tata became Trusts chairman in October 2024. [finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/noel-tata-tata-group-leadership-authority-2026) [businessmodelanalyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/tata-sons-listing-trusts-veto-special-resolution/)
